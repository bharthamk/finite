# WebMCP route replacement and chef-effort acceptance

Date: 2026-08-27

## Accepted claim

Finite changes its advertised WebMCP route only after the tool response that
caused the change has landed. A host that treats registration-signal abort as
execution cancellation therefore cannot erase the triggering result. Explicit
toolset choices invalidate queued automatic refreshes, and the newest completed
route wins.

Canonical ownership also precedes generic stage vocabulary. An arrival waiting
for a human answer remains in the arrival toolset even though its stage is named
`awaiting_human`; it cannot be misrouted into accepted-plan decisions.

## Operator-effort receipt

`finite_get_effort_receipt` is available in the bounded continuity toolset and
returns `finite-chef-effort.v1` with a SHA-256 receipt over the measured adapter
session. It records:

- total tool calls and calls to first useful non-orientation action;
- unique human boundaries rather than repeatedly counting the same question;
- semantic manifest reads and exact selections;
- stale-work and authority refusals;
- cancellation outcomes;
- accepted mutations and failed calls;
- route changes, registry refreshes and refresh failures; and
- current and maximum advertised tool count.

Token use remains `host_owned_unavailable`; Finite does not invent platform
telemetry it cannot observe.

## Adversarial proof

The compatibility host races every route-scoped execution against its
registration abort signal. A travel-to-renovation context switch returns the
complete `PROFILE_SWITCHED` response rather than
`LEGACY_REGISTRATION_ABORTED`; after one event turn the travel tools are absent
and `renovation_replace_material` is present.

The effort journey starts at canonical entry, reaches its first useful travel
action on call three, performs one manifest read and one exact selection,
records one pre-cancelled outcome, crosses a route boundary, stays at or below
20 advertised tools, and produces a receipt whose hash independently
recomputes.

The complete suite passes **128/128**, TypeScript passes, and the production
Vite/Worker build passes. Release target: `operator-reliability-v54`.
