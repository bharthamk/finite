# Native three-family kitchen endurance acceptance

Date: 2026-08-27

## Accepted engineering claim

A native Codex browser can operate three materially different plan families from
human order through accepted plan, ordinary change, late shock, bounded
recovery, external reality, and explicit conclusion without prior Finite
knowledge or an application-owned model. Human authority remains on the Site;
Codex receives one exact continuation route after that authority exists.

The browser registry remains fixed for the document lifetime. Seven native
tools expose a typed semantic dispatcher; changing from arrival to construction,
planning, decisions, evidence, continuity, or plan management changes the
action manifest, not the browser registrations.

## Defects found and repaired during the journeys

- A plan-draft route could inherit the prior active plan's evidence menu. Draft
  confirmation now returns its exact activation arguments and no stale family
  route.
- HMR, same-group refreshes, and cross-group replacement could accumulate native
  registrations. Adapter disposal, route diffing, replacement draining, and the
  fixed dispatcher remove that document-lifetime failure mode.
- Moving semantic actions behind one dispatcher removed the browser's former
  per-tool argument validator. Finite now validates the nested action arguments
  itself against the returned closed schema and refuses missing, coerced,
  out-of-range, enum-invalid, duplicate, or unknown fields before dispatch.
- An accepted plan with no waiting arrival could render the empty arrival page.
  Accepted or closed arrivals no longer outrank an activated plan.
- The Site could directly apply approval and confirmation. It now only creates
  human authority; Codex performs the guarded apply.
- `NO_VALID_OPTION` could fall through to the arrival route and an all-invalid
  menu could appear viable. Both now stay in planning and ask which protected
  boundary may move.
- Construction packet version and tombstone comparisons leaked across projects.
  Regression and resurrection checks are now source-order-local.
- Operator-attributed feedback lacked a revision guard. It now requires the
  expected revision, carries explicit unverified attribution, and refuses stale
  preference compilation.
- Consumer service copy could survive after the state that produced it. Messages
  are now scoped to plan revision, lifecycle, active work, authority, draft, and
  arrival state, then cleared when any of those move.
