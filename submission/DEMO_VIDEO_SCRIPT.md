# Finite final hackathon video script

Target runtime: 1:56.5. Delivery: one continuous founder-style performance at 1.5x pace.

Plans rarely fail when they are made. They fail when reality changes.

Finite turns a plan into a live system Codex can work with. You tell Codex what you want. Finite gives it the current plan, the connections inside it, and the actions that make sense now. You stay in the same plan, comparing and refining the ways forward together.

Think of a restaurant. You are the diner. Codex is the chef. Finite is the kitchen and the service around it. WebMCP lets the chef work inside that kitchen, using the same live plan you can see.

Here is the proof. This eighteen-day trip is already underway. The flights are fixed, Paris matters, and at least five hundred Australian dollars must remain free. Now add three nights in Paris.

Codex enters through WebMCP, reads revision one, and works through everything the change touches. Finite tests twenty-six combinations across the whole plan: calendar, accommodation, transport, commitments and budget. Eighteen fit. Eight do not.

Five meaningfully different directions exist for this plan, so five come back. If only one direction worked, Finite would return one. Each result explains what it protects, what it trades, and why the whole plan still works.

Now the planning continues. I compare the trade-offs with Codex and choose Protect breathing room. Finite shows exactly what that direction would mean: Paris grows from four nights to seven, the trip becomes twenty-one days, the flights still work, and nine hundred and ten dollars remains free.

Once I confirm that direction, Codex applies it to the same live plan. Revision one becomes revision two. The receipt connects the request, the comparison, the decision and every changed value. Reload the page and the new plan is still there. Repeat the apply call and it resolves to the same result.

This is why WebMCP matters. The page publishes typed actions from its current state. Codex reads the plan you are looking at, opens the planning tools it needs, works against the exact revision, and returns the result to the product. As the plan changes, the available actions change with it.

That trip is one kind of plan. The same system can become a weekend away, a dinner for eight, interview preparation, renovation phases, or an event run sheet. The dates, measures, relationships and planning actions adapt to the work.

When one thing changes, you and Codex can see what it affects, explore what still works, and move forward without starting over.

Finite. Change your plans without rebuilding them.
